How to use it

The short version: pick a ready week, cook it for seven days, then start swapping in dishes you actually liked.

The weekday shape

  • One morning cook. Breakfast and the office tiffin come out of the same session — cooker on first, breakfast on the tawa while it whistles.
  • One night cook. Dinner is the lightest meal of the three in every week here.
  • The night-before job. Soaking, or pushing a long-cooking legume through the cooker. Two minutes, and it is what keeps mornings under 40.
  • Weekends are cooked fresh — lunch at home instead of a packed box, and the one slot for something ambitious.

The tiffin box

  • Protein main — dal, legume, soya, paneer, egg or meat. Fill this first.
  • Carb — 1 cup rice or 3 phulka.
  • Veg — a poriyal, thoran or raw salad box.
  • Curd cup — 200 g, in its own container. This single cup is worth 7 g and is what pushes most days past 90.
  • Curd never goes on hot rice. It will be sour by 1 pm.

Reading the marks

  • Green — pure vegetarian.
  • Amber — contains egg.
  • Brown — chicken or mutton.
  • The coloured bar on each card is the region it comes from.
  • The g figure is protein per serving, for a normal home portion.

Things worth knowing

  • Grind sambar and Chettinad masalas in double batches and freeze half. It turns a 40-minute dish into a 20-minute one.
  • Curd goes into anything hot only off the flame. Majjige huli, kadhi, avial — same rule, three states.
  • Squeeze soya chunks dry twice. That is the whole difference between good and inedible.
  • Rice for any bath or chitranna must be cold. Warm rice turns to paste.
